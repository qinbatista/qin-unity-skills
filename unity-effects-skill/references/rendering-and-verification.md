# Rendering and verification

## Bind the renderer and source

Inspect the actual SpriteRenderer, UI, mesh, fullscreen pass or particle host and the installed Unity and rendering package versions. Bind the material and declared render pipeline, graphics API, target device, color space and asset identities. Preserve existing serialized shader properties, default assets and GUIDs. A standalone material check uses the affected material and pass. Effects tied to gameplay also verify the complete composite in the receiving scene.

- For VFX, set anchor and simulation space, capacity, emission, culling bounds, sorting, material and tail. Batch repeated pure decoration inside the effect. Bind start and stop to the real trigger and keep gameplay decisions with their owner.
- For two renders of the same art, preserve the original base draw where required. Match each extra renderer to current Sprite frame, flips, skin/deformation, bones or other anchors, sorting and visibility. Verify actual pass or material-slot behavior in the selected renderer, including transparent ordering and blend accumulation.
- For shader work, use the receiving pipeline's supported HLSL or Shader Graph. Define vertex, UV and texture inputs, units, ranges and color space. Set alpha, blend, depth, culling, queue and required passes. Budget samples, interpolators, variants, precision, texture residency and overdraw at the intended load.
- Reuse shared materials where values agree. Bind per-instance values in the existing owner and release any owned material instance at retirement. Keep camera copies and readbacks to the required passes.
- Give a transparent body and independent emissive line separate coverage where needed. Under straight alpha, normalize premultiplied contributions by safe final coverage in the actual blend contract. Use the line texture's alpha even when transparent pixels carry RGB. Verify visible emission with zero body coverage and the zero-body/zero-emission state with the textures still bound.

## Share rendering calculations

When a code shader and Shader Graph both use a calculation, share it in one guarded HLSL include. Pass inputs explicitly and define the returned opacity, tint and emission contract. Each renderer keeps its own passes, blend, depth and lighting. Verify both callers through the installed Graph's supported function import and compare RGB and alpha at matching fixed and animated inputs.

## Compare the native effect with the original

1. Select the exact design panel or the editable effect's own recorded view that defines the appearance. Label a mechanism-only donor and the derived timing GIF separately. Before reviewing the native take, select the cues that make the original recognizable, the evidence for each and any redesign the brief explicitly allows. A still supports the pictured pose, silhouette and surface. A claim about visual motion needs a timed original showing it.
2. Put the original and final native composite side by side at the corresponding event. Keep both full frames and the same cue cropped from each, with its source page or timestamp, native frame and crop coordinates. Register on the same subject, contact or scene landmark using translation and one uniform scale. Preserve aspect, facing and tonal relationships. Disclose camera or exposure differences. Do not stretch, mirror or recolor evidence to obtain a match. For body-bound cues, mark the relevant palm, head or feet in the original and the live gameplay camera. Compare the origin, contact or shell offset as a fraction of projected bone length, body height or shell diameter. Use that scale for the tolerance and bind placement to the moving rig.
3. At normal viewing size compare the characteristic contour, direction, spacing, palette, texture and edge against the original. Measure the traits that distinguish the cue. A thin arrow and a round spot can have identical color and pixel count. With footage, also compare the path, ordering and intervals. Report **original-to-native fidelity** and **native replay A/B repeatability** independently. A missing or stylistically different required cue fails fidelity even when both replays match the GIF. Mark unavailable evidence unverified. Name the failing cue and phase, fix its Graph, shader or composition, then recapture and compare against the same original.

For overlapping luminous tracks, follow particle age through size, color and alpha, then check their combined brightness after blending at the recorded camera exposure. For contracting rings, sample distinct arcs of the younger, larger ring and the older, smaller one in the same frame at both the dim phase and the peak. Record which arc is brighter in both the original and native take. A matching core or whole-frame total can hide the wrong brightness order.

## Verify internal motion and seed variation

Give each stochastic gameplay cast a fresh detail seed before its first event, including two instances started on the same tick and a returned instance played again. Derive each visual job’s seed from the cast seed and a stable job identifier. Keep actor timing and gameplay events on their own clock. Exercise all three activations through the real trigger and check their effective seeds and visible local differences.

1. Capture onset, development and exit at native size. Track distinct material features in the same regions across successive frames. Measure pairwise distances between identifiable tips or droplets, or fit one rigid rotation and translation to three non-collinear features and inspect the residual motion. For emerging or disappearing features measure the change in occupancy, shape or lifetime. Check against the original at the same phase. Reject a spinning or sliding card when a single transform accounts for the visible motion of a medium that should flow or break up.
2. For preview and CI, apply an explicit fixed seed and its job seeds before the first effect event, including automatic playback on enable. Verify every consuming Graph or shader receives its seed, then capture two fresh native runs at the same trigger, camera and simulation tick. Verify matching silhouette and event times within the declared replay tolerance. Change the seed alone for a third run. Inspect visible, local differences in position, birth, lifetime or surface breakup above fixed-seed variation at gameplay size. Check that the dominant silhouette, direction, density range, contact and retirement still match the source. A common phase or rotation of the whole image does not establish local randomness. Record the seeds, regions, frame times, measurements and each verdict. Repair the failing motion or shading owner and replay the same comparisons.

For example, two outer droplets in a recorded splash nearly tripled their separation in four frame intervals. Rotating the first image preserved that distance even when changing the seed altered most visible pixels. Use the corresponding distances and intervals from the effect under review.

For tracers carried by a pressure front, compare the front's arrival at each tracer with its first deflection. On a shared surface, trigger a second contact while the first wave or spray is visible and follow both tails from their own impact points. For fire, compare the hot core and thin tongues in separate brightness samples and track their positions relative to one another through fuel cutoff. When a sustained field stops emitting, follow the live population through its remaining lifetimes. Sample coverage at the center and edges through the drain to catch a whole field shrinking into a rectangle.

### Verify visible envelopes

Use the [continuous visible envelope rule](staged-effects.md#continuous-visible-envelopes) on two independently started native takes. Isolate each contribution at the receiving design frame rate. At its local origin or advancing front, record consecutive frame times and the visible weight relative to its own off and full values. Include the neighbors of the first and last visible samples and of an interruption, rebind or retrigger. Verify an interior sample at both edges and continuity of the carried weight and phase on both takes. For a negative control, substitute `0, 1, 1, 0` in the same measured region. Reject both instantaneous edges even when the peak and final blank match.

## Observe the actual render path and lifetime

1. Run the affected passes and VFX Graph kernels on the required graphics backend. Inspect shader diagnostics and rendered pixels for errors, NaNs, missing textures or variants, bad normals, clipping, sorting and transparency. Exercise zero and full coverage, UV boundaries, property limits and existing assigned materials.
2. Calibrate visual thresholds on the frozen target at matching scale and phase. If a threshold rejects the intended appearance, correct the measurement before tuning the effect. Compare individual contributions where the visual jobs overlap, using a region that follows the subject. Verify sustained motion separately in its own region. Restore borrowed layer visibility in guaranteed cleanup, then check subject, contact and silhouette readability in the full composite and on a second trigger at the same camera and inputs.
3. Measure native first use separately from warm runs. If first-load rendering consumes an authored phase, verify the relevant readiness in its production owner and preserve the true trigger, timing and cancellation. Record observed frame gaps as well as warmed samples.
4. Capture the referenced native interval and verify stage transitions, retraction, stopped emission and tail retirement. Track a traveling pulse's leading mark along its path over successive early, middle and late frames. At contact, locate the mark on the recipient's rendered artwork in the same frame and repeat with the recipient moving. For pieces that separate, track each visible head relative to contact at native size through launch, spread and fade, then compare individual directions and pairwise distances with the frozen target at matching subject scale and GIF exposure times. A static material can be verified at its declared stable output.
5. Compare CPU and GPU time, allocations, coverage and instance counts at the actual workload and platform. Keep dependency closure, native rendering, lifecycle and performance evidence attributable to the measured scope.

When replacing particles on a borrowed object, trace starts on the host, its children and detached impact pools. A hidden renderer can still simulate. Before activation, save the emission, draw and startup settings, stop and clear the old systems, and guard automatic playback and scripted `Play` or `Emit` for that lease. Through launch and impact, verify each old system reports `IsAlive(false) == false`, `isEmitting == false` and `particleCount == 0`, and inspect the frame for stray output. Restore each slot at its own return, including impacts that outlive the host. Trigger ordinary playback on the same slots and check the saved settings and visible result.
