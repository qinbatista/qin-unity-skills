# Rendering and verification

## Bind the renderer and source

Inspect the actual SpriteRenderer, UI, mesh, fullscreen pass or particle host and the installed Unity and rendering package versions. Bind the material and declared render pipeline, graphics API, target device, color space and asset identities. Preserve existing serialized shader properties, default assets and GUIDs. A standalone material check uses the affected material and pass. Effects tied to gameplay also verify the complete composite in the receiving scene.

- For VFX, set anchor and simulation space, capacity, emission, culling bounds, sorting, material and tail. Batch repeated pure decoration inside the effect. Bind start and stop to the real trigger and keep gameplay decisions with their owner.
- For two renders of the same art, preserve the original base draw where required. Match each extra renderer to current Sprite frame, flips, skin/deformation, bones or other anchors, sorting and visibility. Verify actual pass or material-slot behavior in the selected renderer, including transparent ordering and blend accumulation.
- For an effect surface, [bind the production Shader Graph](graph-and-shader.md#author-the-live-graphs) using the receiving pipeline's supported target. Define vertex, UV and texture inputs, units, ranges and color space. Set alpha, blend, depth, culling, queue and required passes. Budget samples, interpolators, variants, precision, texture residency and overdraw at the intended load.
- Reuse shared materials where values agree. Bind per-instance values in the existing owner and release any owned material instance at retirement. Keep camera copies and readbacks to the required passes.
- Give a transparent body and independent emissive line separate coverage where needed. Under straight alpha, normalize premultiplied contributions by safe final coverage in the actual blend contract. Use the line texture's alpha even when transparent pixels carry RGB. Verify visible emission with zero body coverage and the zero-body/zero-emission state with the textures still bound.

## Share rendering calculations

When Shader Graph callers share a calculation, reuse a Sub Graph or one guarded HLSL include through Custom Function nodes. Pass inputs explicitly and define the returned opacity, tint and emission contract. Each renderer keeps its own blend, depth and lighting. Verify the installed Graph's function import and compare each caller's RGB and alpha at matching fixed and animated inputs.

## Compare the native effect with the original

1. Select the exact design panel or the editable effect's own recorded view that defines the appearance. Label a mechanism-only donor and the derived timing GIF separately. Before reviewing the native take, select the cues that make the original recognizable, the evidence for each and any redesign the brief explicitly allows. A still supports the pictured pose, silhouette and surface. A claim about visual motion needs a timed original showing it.
2. Put the original and final native composite side by side at the corresponding event. Keep both full frames and the same cue cropped from each, with its source page or timestamp, native frame and crop coordinates. Register on the same subject, contact or scene landmark using translation and one uniform scale. Preserve aspect, facing and tonal relationships. Disclose camera or exposure differences. Do not stretch, mirror or recolor evidence to obtain a match. For body-bound cues, mark the relevant palm, head or feet in the original and the live gameplay camera. Compare the origin, contact or shell offset as a fraction of projected bone length, body height or shell diameter. Use that scale for the tolerance and bind placement to the moving rig.
3. At normal viewing size compare the characteristic contour, direction, spacing, palette, texture and edge against the original. Measure the traits that distinguish the cue. A thin arrow and a round spot can have identical color and pixel count. With footage, also compare the path, ordering and intervals. Report **original-to-native fidelity** and **native replay A/B repeatability** independently. A missing or stylistically different required cue fails fidelity even when both replays match the GIF. Mark unavailable evidence unverified. Name the failing cue and phase, fix its Graph, shader or composition, then recapture and compare against the same original.

For overlapping luminous tracks, follow particle age through size, color and alpha, then check their combined brightness after blending at the recorded camera exposure. For contracting rings, sample distinct arcs of the younger, larger ring and the older, smaller one in the same frame at both the dim phase and the peak. Record which arc is brighter in both the original and native take. A matching core or whole-frame total can hide the wrong brightness order.

### Trace an unexplained straight edge

1. Register the reported image to its native frame using a scene landmark, uniform scale and the event clock. Inspect the neighboring frames and the same region during formation, established flow, contact and release. A cut that persists after closure has a different cause from a squared-off advancing tip.
2. Isolate the contributing draw. Compare its RGB and alpha on light and dark backgrounds with the uncropped artwork. Trace any actor-shaped hole, ground extraction line or atlas border. If the sampled material is continuous, inspect the reveal mask, UV limits, mesh bounds, stencil, depth and actual scene contact. Find which space the edge follows as the material or camera moves.
3. [Reconstruct the affected material](staged-effects.md#continue-a-free-surface-through-occlusion), then capture the same region at gameplay scale and at a readable magnification. Put the rejected and revised clips on one clock. Show two independent revised native replays and the composite with its real occluders. Keep the original silhouette and local motion visible outside the repair.
4. Measure the reported defect locally. For a closed liquid rim, sample across its radial thickness and measure the longest contiguous arc lacking the strand signal seen on its flanks. Check that the interior remains open and that strands still travel during the hold. Calibrate on the reported cut and on an intact stretch. Record the frames, scale and values for both runs. Preserve intentional contacts and leave visual sign-off with the reviewer.

## Verify local motion per object

Register the target GIF, original and two independent native replays to the same event, view and scale. Before measuring, name each job's active interval, reference frame and visible landmark from the [motion inventory](staged-effects.md#decompose-every-visible-object). A still supplies anatomy, palette and pose. Label the inferred movement and compare it with the timed study.

- Isolate the real draw or neutralize that job's bound driver while its neighbors continue. Restore it before the next capture. Track entry, sustained motion, response, retirement and reuse. Preserve the authored clock through holds and reversals.
- When subjects overlap, identify the affected pixels or emitter and verify that its neighbor keeps its original contact frame. A direction control must produce trackable motion in the wrong direction. Fill any area exposed by a test transform with the actual camera backdrop.
- For dim or sparse effects, compare lossless RGB captures against the native empty backdrop and read coverage over the full cast before encoding. Check the delivered movie separately. A small codec shift over a large empty area can outweigh the light from the effect.
- When a fading particle seems to move a few pixels, compare its brightness-normalized image patch at the measured displacement and at its original position. A thresholded centroid can move on a stationary shape as its edges fade. Challenge the tracker with that stationary fading shape.
- Record a native solo exposure and an empty backdrop for every required draw during its expected visible interval. A required shaft or child with zero pixels fails even when adjacent particles draw, the shader imports, and the composite test passes. Compare the solo footprint with its expected emitter or attachment in screen space.
- Remove camera and parent transforms when testing a child. For a wing, register on the thorax and measure each tip against its own hinge. For a rotating or translating card, fit the rigid transform and compare the remaining silhouette change. For a rigid projectile, measure its own trajectory, angular response or contact in the world frame called for by the brief. For a beam, hold the endpoints fixed and track a crest's travel between two stations.
- Run a frozen-object control with the rest of the composite alive. Also freeze a child's local driver while its parent travels or rotates. For a layered cast, perform the converse by moving the guide while freezing the shaft material. Each control must fail its own measurement. Sample multiple frames spanning a stroke or transit, calibrate at gameplay scale and inspect the composite beside the original. Luminance flicker, random frame noise and aggregate particle counts do not establish these motions.
- For a small colored landmark, compare a lossless native RGB exposure and the delivered animation at the same pose. Track its color and area inside the moving tissue. Chroma subsampling or a global GIF palette can erase a few pixels even when a silhouette measurement passes. Regenerate the review from native RGB and inspect the encoded result at its published size.
- Record per-job values and the corresponding frames for both runs. Vary the seed to check local detail and repeat the true gameplay trigger. Return to the smallest failing job, repair and replay. Keep the user's visual decision separate from these checks.

## Verify internal motion and seed variation

Give each stochastic gameplay cast a fresh detail seed before its first event, including two instances started on the same tick and a returned instance played again. Derive each visual job’s seed from the cast seed and a stable job identifier. Keep actor timing and gameplay events on their own clock. Exercise all three activations through the real trigger and check their effective seeds and visible local differences.

1. Capture onset, development and exit at native size. Track distinct material features in the same regions across successive frames. Measure pairwise distances between identifiable tips or droplets, or fit one rigid rotation and translation to three non-collinear features and inspect the residual motion. For emerging or disappearing features measure the change in occupancy, shape or lifetime. Check against the original at the same phase. Reject a spinning or sliding card when a single transform accounts for the visible motion of a medium that should flow or break up.
2. For preview and CI, apply an explicit fixed seed and its job seeds before the first effect event, including automatic playback on enable. Verify every consuming Graph or shader receives its seed, then capture two fresh native runs at the same trigger, camera and simulation tick. Verify matching silhouette and event times within the declared replay tolerance. Change the seed alone for a third run. Inspect visible, local differences in position, birth, lifetime or surface breakup above fixed-seed variation at gameplay size. Check that the dominant silhouette, direction, density range, contact and retirement still match the source. A common phase or rotation of the whole image does not establish local randomness. Record the seeds, regions, frame times, measurements and each verdict. Repair the failing motion or shading owner and replay the same comparisons.

For example, two outer droplets in a recorded splash nearly tripled their separation in four frame intervals. Rotating the first image preserved that distance even when changing the seed altered most visible pixels. Use the corresponding distances and intervals from the effect under review.

For tracers carried by a pressure front, compare the front's arrival at each tracer with its first deflection. On a shared surface, trigger a second contact while the first tail coasts. Identify the event that births each response and measure the signed contribution at the trough between impacts against its preceding peak. Isolate contact light with a same-frame draw omission and sample neighboring strips on both sides of the impact. A passing beam can brighten one strip before any off-axis contact light appears. For fire, compare the hot core and thin tongues in separate brightness samples and track their positions relative to one another through fuel cutoff. When a sustained field stops emitting, follow the live population through its remaining lifetimes. Sample coverage at the center and edges through the drain to catch a whole field shrinking into a rectangle.

### Verify visible envelopes

Apply the [visible envelope rule](staged-effects.md#continuous-visible-envelopes) to the frozen GIF at its original exposure times and playback speed. Resample it at each receiving design frame rate over every distinct sampling phase (two for a 60 Hz study at 30 Hz). For irregular holds, test every interval between exposure boundaries modulo the receiver frame period and include the boundaries. An intermediate must survive every phase.

On two independently started native takes, isolate each contribution and measure consecutive rendered brightness or coverage at the same local point as the front passes. Normalize against that contribution’s own off and full output. A whole-frame light sum can stay smooth while this point jumps. Include the neighbors of the first and last visible samples and of an interruption, rebind or retrigger. Verify an interior sample at both edges and continuity of the carried weight and phase on both takes. For a negative control, substitute `0, 1, 1, 0` in the same measured region. Reject both instantaneous edges even when the peak and final blank match.

## Observe the actual render path and lifetime

For a [single-subject revision](../SKILL.md#scope-one-subject) inside a larger effect, use matched-time captures of the subject alone, the surrounding context with that subject omitted, and the intact receiver. Verify that the intended cue changes and that other draws keep their footprint, events and timing. Treat any requested approval as a separate result.

1. Run the affected passes and VFX Graph kernels on the required graphics backend. Inspect shader diagnostics and rendered pixels for errors, NaNs, missing textures or variants, bad normals, clipping, sorting and transparency. Exercise zero and full coverage, UV boundaries, property limits and existing assigned materials.
2. Calibrate visual thresholds on the frozen target at matching scale and phase. If a threshold rejects the intended appearance, correct the measurement before tuning the effect. Compare individual contributions where the visual jobs overlap, using a region that follows the subject. Verify sustained motion separately in its own region. Restore borrowed layer visibility in guaranteed cleanup, then check subject, contact and silhouette readability in the full composite and on a second trigger at the same camera and inputs.
3. Measure native first use separately from warm runs. If first-load rendering consumes an authored phase, verify the relevant readiness in its production owner and preserve the true trigger, timing and cancellation. Record observed frame gaps as well as warmed samples.
4. Capture the referenced native interval and verify stage transitions, retraction, stopped emission and tail retirement. Track a traveling pulse's leading mark along its path over successive early, middle and late frames. At contact, locate the mark on the recipient's rendered artwork in the same frame and repeat with the recipient moving. For pieces that separate, track each visible head relative to contact at native size through launch, spread and fade, then compare individual directions and pairwise distances with the frozen target at matching subject scale and GIF exposure times. When a surface parameter stays constant, verify the object's other visible motion during that interval.
5. Compare CPU and GPU time, allocations, coverage and instance counts at the actual workload and platform. Keep dependency closure, native rendering, lifecycle and performance evidence attributable to the measured scope.

When replacing particles on a borrowed object, trace starts on the host, its children and detached impact pools. A hidden renderer can still simulate. Before activation, save the emission, draw and startup settings, stop and clear the old systems, and guard automatic playback and scripted `Play` or `Emit` for that lease. Through launch and impact, verify each old system reports `IsAlive(false) == false`, `isEmitting == false` and `particleCount == 0`, and inspect the frame for stray output. Restore each slot at its own return, including impacts that outlive the host. Trigger ordinary playback on the same slots and check the saved settings and visible result.
